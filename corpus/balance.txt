BALANCE AND MOVEMENT — FOUNDATION

PHILOSOPHY
Everything starts with balanced movement. Without 
balance, everything else the student performs is 
redundant. A balanced player can correctly position 
themselves relative to the ball and perform complex 
actions to manipulate it. Balance is taught before 
any technique — a student must be able to move with 
balance before they swing at the ball.

PROGRESSION — complete each before moving on:
1. Student moves to the ball with balance, no swing.
2. Student balances on both feet whilst hitting.
3. Student balances on either foot whilst hitting.

WHAT BALANCED MOVEMENT LOOKS LIKE:
- Chest upright before, during, and after the shot.
- Head still — eyes track the ball all the way to 
  racket contact, not lifting early to see where 
  the ball goes.
- Weight on balls of feet, knees slightly bent in 
  ready position.
- After the shot, player can hold their finish 
  position without stumbling.

COMMON BALANCE ERRORS IN TENNIS:
- Moving feet, torso or head during the swing: 
  caused by late positioning — the student is still 
  arriving at the ball when they swing, so the body 
  is still in motion. Fix: get there early, stop, 
  then swing.
- Torso collapsing after the shot: caused by rushing 
  to recover before finishing the swing, or weak. 
  core engagement through contact. Fix: think about finishing 
  the shot before recovering back.

CUES FOR BALANCE:
- "Hold your finish until the your shot bounces"
- "Pretend you're standing on a surfboard — 
   stay centred"
- "Move your feet, then swing — not at the same time"

DRILLS FOR BALANCE:
Drill 1 — Stop and Swing:
Coach feeds balls to various positions. Student 
must move to the ball, stop both feet completely, 
swing, and freeze the finish position until the 
ball lands on the other side. If the student moves 
during the swing the rep doesn't count. Start slow, 
increase feed pace as balance improves.
